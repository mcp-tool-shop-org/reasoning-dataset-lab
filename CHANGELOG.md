## [0.6.0] - 2026-11-07

**The bake-off launcher is ready for its paid windows: it binds each window to its part, checks every boot input before it spends, records the boot gatekeeper, reads the window's own rate and keeps re-reading it, and books nothing when no machine was assigned.**

### Mid-run rate re-reads

- **The launcher keeps reading the rate while the run cell runs.** The cost-cap monitor in `scripts/colab/launch_judges.sh` re-reads `colab usage` every `RDLAB_RATE_REREAD_SECONDS` (1 to 60, default 30; anything else refuses `BAD_RATE_REREAD_INTERVAL` before any `colab` call). Each read runs under `timeout -k` and is parsed for the account rate and the "Active assignments" count. The rules live in `scripts/colab/rate_rereads.py`.
- **Our rate is the account rate minus a neighbour baseline.** The baseline starts as the pre-open account rate.
- **A change in the assignment count is never judged.** When the count changes, the launcher waits for two consecutive reads at the new count that agree within 0.01. It then sets the baseline to the account rate minus the highest stable rate of ours seen. That is the safe direction: our apparent rate can only come out higher. A neighbour that opens or closes mid-run therefore neither trips the window nor lowers our rate.
- **Only a stable read at the settled count can raise our rate.** A rise reprices the remaining runtime at max(`--max-rate`, the highest stable rate seen). The runtime can shorten, never lengthen. A stable rate above `--max-rate` ends the window `RATE_ABOVE_MAX` mid-run: the monitor fetches the results and stops the run as it does on a cost-cap stop, and `status.json` and the outcome say `stopped_rate_reread`.
- **An in-flight read never outlives the window.** Each re-read runs as a tracked background PID. If the run ends while a read is in flight, the monitor's trap and the launcher's teardown kill that PID and its children by PID, never by name.
- **It fails closed.** Once the window is open, a read with no count, a count of 0 or no rate is recorded as a bad read. So is a read whose own rate, at the settled count, is below the card's floor (`GPU_RATE_FLOOR` on a GPU card); such a rate is never accepted. Three bad reads in a row end the window `ASSIGNMENTS_UNREADABLE`. This closes the case where a neighbour closes and the rate drops two reads before the count: our stable 0.00 used to be accepted, the next re-baseline then hid our later rise. It is not a known limit.
- **Before the first stable read, a count of 0 means "not yet".** The real service reports `Usage rate: 0.00/hr` with `Active assignments: 0` until a new session registers (windowC2-12-t4-s1a-20261027). At open, such a read only uses up the open-poll budget, and the window rate must come from two agreeing reads at the same count of at least 1. A window that never gets there refuses `LIVE_USAGE_RATE_UNAVAILABLE`.
- **Every read is recorded.** Each re-read's account rate, count, our rate, baseline, decision and remaining runtime go to `rate_rereads.jsonl` and to `rate_rereads` in `outcome.draft.json`. The usage text itself is not stored there, so no token can be. A stable rise books the cost at the highest stable rate seen, and `pricing_rate_per_hour` reports the final pricing rate.
- **Known limits**, documented in `docs/colab-cli-contract.md` and `scripts/colab/REAL_SERVICE_ASSUMPTIONS.md`: a neighbour that opens in the same read as our own rate rises folds that rise into the baseline; a CLI outage of three or more reads ends the window early; a count that leads the neighbour's rate by two or more reads can trip falsely.
- The fake CLI prints `colab usage` in the real line format. `FAKE_COLAB_USAGE_SEQUENCE` models the rate and count of each call during the whole window, the recorded T4 open is the fixture `t4_open_0001` (`FAKE_COLAB_USAGE_FIXTURE`), and `FAKE_COLAB_EXEC_RUN_DELAY` keeps a run cell alive for a while before it finishes normally. `FAKE_COLAB_USAGE_SLOW_FROM_CALL` and `FAKE_COLAB_USAGE_SLOW_SECONDS` make a usage call hang.

### Lock-check in two windows, P0a per judge in child venvs, LC-1 anchors

- **Lock-check in two windows: `--lock-check-phase anchors|p0a|all`** (launcher and VM, no default; `LOCK_CHECK_PHASE_REQUIRED` / `LOCK_CHECK_PHASE_INVALID` before any colab call or paid step). `anchors` records the boot gatekeeper goldens and runs the five judges over the 50 anchors (no P0a); `p0a` runs P0a only; `all` runs both as before. Default wall per phase 3600 / 5400 / 7200 s, so `--dry` prices the phase; `--dry` prints a `[lock-check] phase=...` line. The receipts, the summary, the filled goldens' provenance and the outcome draft record the phase. `promote_goldens.py` takes the `anchors` (or `all`, or pre-split) window's goldens and refuses a `p0a` window's (`PROMOTE_GOLDENS_PHASE_REFUSED`).
- **`rdlab bakeoff-lockcheck-anchors --part-dir <counted part> --out <file>`**: the 50 label-blind LC-1 anchors (rule `rdlab-lc1-anchors-v1`: the counted part minus the 30 determinism canaries; key `sha256(seed|id)`; five per domain on distinct triads, ten domains; sorted by key) as the upload projection, with `<file>.sha256` and a manifest (rule, seed, pool size, sorted-id SHA-256, per-domain and per-triad counts). It reads only `id`, `domain` and the SHA-256 of `material` (the triad grouping) and refuses `LC1_ANCHORS_SPLIT_IMPOSSIBLE` when 10 x 5 on distinct triads cannot be met. The anchor half once called "canaries" is now named the low-margin anchors in docs and help (`select_lock_check_low_margin_anchors`); JSON field names are unchanged.
- **Lock-check P0a runs one judge at a time, each in its own venv child process** (`vm_judges.run_lock_check_p0a_in_children`; `bakeoff_judge --p0a-stage passes|rerun`). Before, VM `main` built a real transport for all five judges in its own process and kept them (about 51 GB of peaks on a 40 GB A100), and d3-mini's transformers 5.17.0 and the others' 5.19.0 cannot share one process. The parent now only checks, schedules and collects; `p0a_report.json` keeps its format, and `p0a_children.json` records each child.
- **A failed P0a child no longer loses the finished children's work.** Each child's output is written to `p0a_children/<judge>.<stage>.json` under the run dir (on the result tar's allow-list), so it is fetched with the run. When a child fails, the refusal class is unchanged, the refusal's `completed_judges` lists the finished children, and a partial `p0a_children.json` (`status: failed`) names the failed judge, its stage and its class and lists each finished child with its output SHA-256.

### The rate contract

- **The window's rate, not the account's.** `colab usage` reports the rate of every open session on the account. The launcher reads it before `colab new` and after, and takes the window's rate as post minus pre. A rate is accepted only when two reads 5 s apart agree within 0.01 and clear the floor: `GPU_RATE_FLOOR` is 1.00/h on a GPU card (the T4 measured 1.07/h; CPU sessions are at most 0.26/h). Otherwise the window refuses `LIVE_USAGE_RATE_UNAVAILABLE`, and an unreadable pre-open rate refuses before any spend.
- **`--max-rate <units/h>` (`RDLAB_MAX_RATE`) is required** for `--time-one`, `--lock-check` and `--counted`, with no default (`MAX_RATE_REQUIRED` before any `colab` call, `BAD_MAX_RATE` if it is not positive). A window rate above it refuses `RATE_ABOVE_MAX` and the session is stopped.
- **The cap runtime is priced on max(`--max-rate`, the window rate)**, so an under-read can only shorten a window. The cost clock starts when `colab new` succeeds, so the polls count against the cap. Every usage read is bounded by `timeout -k`.
- **No rate constant.** The launcher used to assume 5.30/h for an A100 and 0.08/h for CPU; the A100 is 6.73/h, and 8.88/h with High-RAM. The only rate recorded now is the live one. Before the read, `--dry` prints a `[rate]` projection from `--max-rate`, labelled an estimate. A cost booked before the read is `max_rate_x_runtime_estimate`, or `null` (`rate_not_read`) without `--max-rate`.
- The outcome draft records `rate_scope: window`, `pre_account_rate_per_hour`, `post_account_rate_per_hour`, `pricing_rate_per_hour` and `max_rate`, and `max_rate` is forwarded to the VM for `time_one_summary.json`.
- Every `timeout`-wrapped colab call now kills a client that ignores SIGTERM after one grace, `COLAB_KILL_GRACE_SECONDS` (`RDLAB_COLAB_KILL_GRACE_SECONDS`, default 15; `BAD_KILL_GRACE` if it is not a positive integer).

### No capacity

- **No session assigned costs nothing.** When `colab new` fails because the service refused the machine at assign (`POST .../tun/m/assign...: Service Unavailable`) and the session is listed neither before nor after, `outcome.draft.json` books `cost: 0` with `cost_basis: no_session_assigned`, `session_assigned: false` and `failure_class: no_capacity`. `colab-cli.log` gets a `NO_CAPACITY` line and the launcher prints an `[outcome]` summary line on stderr. timeone-mu2 (2026-11-06) had booked 0.0927 for a window that never got a machine.
- A session that is listed after a failed `colab new` was assigned. The launcher adopts and stops it, and books rate x runtime (`cost_basis: rate_x_runtime`, `SESSION_OPEN_FAILED_AFTER_ASSIGN`).

### Part binding

- **Real windows read the part's own files.** A part built by this repo holds `checksums.txt`, `items.jsonl`, `part.json` and `provenance.jsonl`, and no builder writes a part `manifest.json`. The launcher and VM used to look only for one, so every real window on a real part refused `PART_MANIFEST_MISSING`, and a hand-written manifest of another part was accepted with nothing tying it to the items.
- **`launch_judges.sh --part-dir DIR` (`RDLAB_PART_DIR`) names the part**, and is required for a real `--time-one` or `--counted` (`PART_MANIFEST_MISSING` before any `colab` call). Before any `colab` call, `part.json` must match the part schema and its `checksums.txt` line, the part's `items.jsonl` must match its line and hold `items_count` rows, and every uploaded row must equal the part row with the same id (`id`, `material`, `claim`). A counted window uploads the whole part in order; a time-one window may upload any subset. `part.json` and `checksums.txt` are staged byte for byte beside `items.jsonl`. A mismatch refuses `PART_MANIFEST_ITEMS_MISMATCH`, which names the field and never the item text.
- **VM `main` runs the same check** at `verify_part_manifest`, before any paid step, and deletes and lists the staged files after the run.
- **`rdlab bakeoff-project --part-dir <part> --out <file>`** writes a part's Colab upload projection (`id`, `material`, `claim`, every item in the part's order) and `<file>.sha256`, after checking the part against its `checksums.txt`. It never writes inside the part, under a `sealed_store/` (`PROJECTION_OUT_FORBIDDEN`) or over an existing file (`PROJECTION_OUT_EXISTS`), and refuses a part whose files no longer match (`PROJECTION_PART_INVALID`).
- **The receipts show both checks, labelled.** The launcher writes its row-by-row binding to `launcher_part_binding.json`, logs it, puts it in `outcome.draft.json` and uploads it. The VM checks that it names this part and this upload (`PART_BINDING_RECORD_MISMATCH` otherwise). In the receipts, `part_identity.binding` is the rig's result (`checked_by: "rig"`, `projection_checked: true`) with the VM's re-check beside it (`checked_by: "vm"`). Without a record, the VM's binding says `projection_checked: null` with the reason. Nothing defaults to `false`.
- **Part pins and the seal.** `frozen_mac` is an HMAC under the sealed store's key, so it cannot be checked outside the store, and the receipts say so (`seal.frozen_mac_verified: false`). A real window needs `frozen: true` with a 64-hex `frozen_mac` (`PART_NOT_FROZEN`), the launcher checks every file `checksums.txt` lists, and the burned pilot and the anchored counted part are pinned by their `part.json` and `items.jsonl` SHA-256. An edited and re-summed pinned part refuses `PART_PIN_MISMATCH`. Other parts are recorded `unpinned_trust_on_first_use`.
- Receipts and `time_one_summary.json` record `part_identity` (the real `part_id`, `items_count`, both SHA-256 and the binding rule) instead of `part_manifest`. **Receipts written before this change carry `part_id` `"pilot-40b"` for the burned pilot**, a label the VM made up; its real `part_id` is `bakeoff-d-t10-sealed`.
- **The repo bundle is the git-tracked files at a clean HEAD.** The launcher used to tar every file under `src/` and `scripts/colab/`, so an untracked file there would have been uploaded. It now bundles `git ls-files` of those paths and refuses `REPO_TREE_DIRTY` when any is modified, staged or untracked (`REPO_TREE_UNREADABLE` outside a git work tree).

### The boot gatekeeper

- **The download set.** Every real `--time-one`, `--counted` and `--lock-check` window downloads and verifies the boot gatekeeper's judge (`nemotron-3-nano-4b`, the goldens' `judge_id`), once, and records it as `boot_gatekeeper_judge`. timeone-mu (2026-11-06) had fetched only mu-judge and then failed `BAKEOFF_JUDGE_SNAPSHOT_NOT_FOUND` after the paid setup. `--dry` prints the set as a `[judges]` line. A window whose gatekeeper judge is not in the set, or was not verified, refuses `BOOT_GATEKEEPER_JUDGE_NOT_DOWNLOADED`.
- **The receipts preflight.** Every judge in the download set has its receipt checked before any spend: present, valid JSON with a `files` mapping, and `repo` and `revision` equal to the pin (`BAKEOFF_JUDGE_RECEIPT_NOT_FOUND`, `_INVALID`, `_MISMATCH`). The launcher runs it before any `colab` call (`--dry` prints a `[receipts]` line) and VM `main` before venv sync. Only the gatekeeper's receipt used to be checked, and only for presence.
- **Record mode for time-one.** A time-one window runs the gatekeeper in record mode: it writes `boot_gatekeeper_goldens.filled.json`, compares nothing, and says `gatekeeper: recorded, not compared`, with `expected_output_token_ids_sha256: null`. Before, every real time-one refused `BOOT_GATEKEEPER_GOLDEN_MISSING`, because the repo's goldens are unfilled. Counted windows still compare, and their "no golden for this card" refusal now fires before any paid step. No window writes `scripts/colab/boot_gatekeeper_goldens.json`.
- **The card type comes from the opened session.** The launcher reads `Hardware:` and `Shape:` from `colab sessions` after `colab new`, maps them with a table pinned to receipts (A100 Standard is `A100`, A100 High-RAM is `A100-80`, plus G4, L4 and T4 Standard) and forwards the result as `RDLAB_CARD_TYPE`. An unknown card refuses `CARD_TYPE_UNKNOWN` right after open. VM `main` checks it against `nvidia-smi` before venv sync (`CARD_TYPE_MISMATCH`), and `vm_receipt.json` records the card it ran on (`hardware`, `gpu_name`, `hardware_source`) instead of always saying `A100`.
- **Phase timings.** The gatekeeper's subprocess timeout is 300 s (it was 120 s). The subprocess times `torch_import`, `verify_ready`, `model_load`, a labelled `warmup`, `generation_1`, `generation_2` and `total`, and prints one `BOOT_GATEKEEPER_PHASE` line per phase, so a timeout shows how far it got. The timings are in the gatekeeper report as `subprocess_timings`.
- **Token count.** Each prompt records its `output_token_count` and effective `max_new_tokens`. The gatekeeper's judge is a one-token judge, so any other count refuses `BOOT_GATEKEEPER_TOKEN_COUNT_MISMATCH`. A judge venv without torch refuses `BAKEOFF_JUDGE_DEPS_MISSING` before the weights are re-hashed.
- A subprocess refusal keeps the exception: `failure_output_tail` (the last 40 lines, scrubbed) and `exception_line` go to `status.json` and `vm_receipt.json`, with `VM_REFUSAL_*` lines in `vm.log`. A gatekeeper timeout is a `BOOT_GATEKEEPER_SUBPROCESS_FAILED` refusal.
- `scripts/colab/local_boot_gatekeeper.py` runs the gatekeeper as the VM does against a local HF home on a GPU, prints each prompt's output hash and whether it matches the goldens for `--card-type`, and writes a JSON receipt. It refuses a CPU device, and an `--out` inside the repository or over an existing file without `--overwrite` (`LOCAL_GATEKEEPER_OUT_REFUSED`).

### The single-kernel contract

- **`colab exec` runs one cell at a time per session** (`docs/colab-cli-contract.md`, with the CLI's source lines). An exec issued while an earlier cell still runs waits behind it, and killing the local client does not stop the cell. The alive probe therefore runs a bounded `colab restart-kernel -s` (`RDLAB_RESTART_KERNEL_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`) before it retries an attempt that timed out or lost its connection, and logs `ALIVE_PROBE_KERNEL_RESTART`.
- The fake CLI models the contract: execs queue per session behind a running cell, a timed-out exec leaves its cell running, `restart-kernel` ends it, uploads and downloads do not wait, and each exec's code and outputs go to a history file. Its per-session state files are rewritten under a lock.

### Counted refusals

- **A counted window runs only on a pinned part.** A part that matches no pin refuses `PART_NOT_PINNED`, in the launcher before any `colab` call and in VM `main` before venv sync. Time-one and lock-check windows keep trust on first use.
- **A counted window never runs on the burned pilot** (`PART_IS_BURNED_PILOT`), whether the pilot is named by its hashes, its pin name or its item-id set (`PILOT_40B_ITEM_IDS_SHA256`).
- **Stub goldens refuse in the free gates.** Goldens marked `synthetic_fake_hash`, `stub_transport` or a stub provenance refuse `BOOT_GATEKEEPER_STUB_TRANSPORT_REFUSED` in the launcher and in VM `main` before venv sync, instead of inside the gatekeeper after the weight download.
- **Test hooks.** The launcher checks the variables it forwards for test hooks before any `colab` call (`TEST_HOOK_IN_COUNTED_MODE`).
- **Item limits.** `RDLAB_LIMIT_ITEMS` refuses in a real counted window (`COUNTED_LIMIT_ITEMS_REFUSED`), in the launcher and in VM `main`.

### `promote_goldens` safe default

- `scripts/colab/promote_goldens.py` checks a window's filled boot gatekeeper goldens and writes a candidate `boot_gatekeeper_goldens.json` outside every git work tree, for review. It refuses stub goldens by default (`PROMOTE_GOLDENS_STUB_REFUSED`). `--allow-stub-transport` is for tests only, and `--forbid-stub-transport` is kept as an accepted no-op.

### Bake-off launcher fixes (#66 to #71)

- **The lock-check window** (#66). `launch_judges.sh --lock-check` (A100) and `--lock-check-stub` (CPU) take `--anchor-items` (the 50 anchors from the counted part), `--p0a-items` (the 114 burned pilot items), `--p0a-part-dir` (`RDLAB_P0A_PART_DIR`; the pilot's sealed part) and one counted source, `--counted-items` or `--counted-manifest`. The window records the gatekeeper goldens, runs the five judges over the anchors, writes `golden_anchor_manifest.json` and a `shard_manifest.json` built from the whole counted part, and runs P0a (`p0a_report.json`). Both input sets are checked before a session opens and deleted on the VM after the run.
- **The pilot is named by its pinned hashes, never by a label.** P0a and `rdlab bakeoff-logprob-mass` used to require the made-up label `pilot-40b`, which the real pilot does not carry, so a real lock-check would have refused. Both now read the pilot part's `part.json` and `checksums.txt` and require the pinned pilot SHA-256 (`P0A_PILOT_IDENTITY_MISSING`, `P0A_PILOT_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`). A lock-check window's `pilot_mass_check` is `not_applicable`.
- **Refuse before spend** (#68, #69). The counted source, the lock-check inputs, the part and the judge and gatekeeper preflights are all checked in the launcher (`--dry` refuses whatever live refuses) and in VM `main`, before venv sync, the device and GPU gate and the weight download. New refusals: `PART_MANIFEST_MISSING`, `PART_MANIFEST_INVALID`, `PART_MANIFEST_SYNTHESIZED`, `COUNTED_ITEMS_REQUIRED`, `LOCK_CHECK_COUNTED_SOURCE_INVALID`, `SHARD_MANIFEST_GOLDEN_SHA_MISMATCH` and `SHARD_MANIFEST_GOLDEN_SHA_MISSING`. The shard manifest records the golden file's SHA-256 as `golden_anchor_manifest_file_sha256`.
- **The free gate before any paid window** (#69, #70). `test_e2e_fake_real_mode_time_one_reaches_judges` and `test_e2e_fake_real_mode_counted_reaches_judges` drive the real launcher in real mode against the fake CLI, through every preflight and into the judge step, on a part built by the real builder. The fake VM cell runs under a guard that refuses reads of the host. Both must pass before a paid counted or time-one window is booked. They do not prove what only a GPU VM shows; the first paid time-one window is the rung for that. An opt-in rung runs the same path on the real projected pilot items.
- **`time_one_summary.json` records this launch's limits** (`wall_seconds`, `vm_wall_seconds`, `cap_units`, forwarded by the launcher) instead of the defaults.
- GitHub tokens are scrubbed from every log and receipt, as HF, OAuth, JWT and colab proxy tokens already were.
- `launch_judges.sh` and `vm_judges.py` are executable again (100755), with a test on the modes of the scripts that are run directly.
- The pinned-uv tests no longer share `/tmp/uv-pinned` across workers (`FAKE_COLAB_PINNED_UV_DIR`).
- `scripts/colab/REAL_SERVICE_ASSUMPTIONS.md` row C12 cites the right lines, and the test-hook table lists the fake CLI's hooks.

## [0.5.0] - 2026-11-05

**The judge bake-off: build a sealed test part, run five local judges on it, score them without anyone seeing the keys first, and run the judges on Colab.** Tests: 1,162 passing, 2 skipped.

### Added

- **`rdlab bakeoff-part`** builds the writer-only Kind-D (T10) judge bake-off part from certified value sets, into an output directory outside the repo, with a sealed key store. `--resume` continues from a per-triad sealed checkpoint. `--construction anchored-two-hop` builds the anchored two-hop variant (domains, closures, entities and anchors rotate).
- **`rdlab bakeoff-probe`** runs the out-of-fold shortcut probe and the role and closure class-share gates on a part, so a part whose answers can be guessed from surface features fails before any judge sees it.
- **`rdlab bakeoff-judge`** runs one judge over the part's items without opening the sealed store, then closes its outputs into `outputs-closed.json`.
- **`rdlab bakeoff-judge-score`** verifies the closed outputs, joins them with the sealed keys, and writes sealed per-row records plus public aggregates. It refuses rows without timings (`BAKEOFF_SCORE_MISSING_ROW_SECONDS`) and parts that mix sources (`BAKEOFF_SCORE_MIXED_PART`).
- **`rdlab bakeoff-canary-tolerance`** measures the empirical log-prob drift of a 10-item canary set over repeated runs, and **`rdlab bakeoff-logprob-mass`** checks that yes/no probability mass covers at least 0.99 for binary calls.
- **Winner selection follows the pre-registration exactly.** No lenient fallbacks: explicit lower confidence bounds, per-class precision, pairwise rows, eligibility floors decided before the Holm candidate set, and registered domains only.
- **Colab launcher for the five-judge bake-off** (`scripts/colab/launch_judges.sh`). Each judge runs as its own subprocess in a hash-locked venv on CPython 3.15.1 with a pinned uv. The launcher checks the GPU, the disk and the device before any download, records step and per-judge timings with memory telemetry, caps spend from the live Colab rate, and stops its own session. Modes: `--session-smoke`, `--probe`, `--stub`, `--time-one`, `--counted` and `--dry`, plus `--resume` and `--until STEP` for stubs. Timing windows on A100s have run with it; see `REAL_SERVICE_ASSUMPTIONS.md` for what each real-service step relies on.
- New environment variables: `RDLAB_JUDGE_ORDER`, `RDLAB_FIRST_LOAD_JUDGE_ID`, `RDLAB_ONLY_JUDGES` and `RDLAB_FORCE_PINNED_UV`. `REQUEST_TIMEOUT` now defaults to 60 s.

### Experimental

- **Sharded counted windows** (`--shard k/n`, `--shard-manifest`, `--loop subblock|judge_outer`, `--subblock-items`, and `scripts/colab/merge_shards.py`). The merge refuses on any real-mode guard failure (including test hooks or overrides set in the environment), a determinism mismatch, a missing or changed golden anchor, missing ECC fields or any uncorrected ECC error, or verdict flips above the anchor budget, and it reports output-token drift as a warning. **No counted sharded run has completed yet.** The lock-check window that records the golden anchors is still in review and not in this release, so a counted run refuses at boot until it lands.

### Changed

- CI test legs time out at 25 minutes and run under pytest-xdist. Tests use committed fixtures instead of git history or the network.

### Changed

- **certify-values: `--reader-think FAMILY=SETTING`, `--parent-manifest PATH`, `rdlab pilot-b compare-arms`, and Nemotron pin `think="medium"`.** `rdlab pilot-b certify-values` gains repeatable `--reader-think FAMILY=SETTING` (`true`, `false`, `low`, `medium`, `high`, `max`) to override the pinned `think` for that reader family only (refusing unknown families or settings with exit code 2 before any Ollama call) and records `"think_overridden": true` on overridden reader rows. `--parent-manifest PATH` records `parent_manifest_sha256` (sha256 of the parent file's bytes) and `setting_tier` (e.g. `"nemotron:think=medium"`), refuses to overwrite the parent manifest or its companion review queue (`PILOT_B_CERT_PARENT_OVERWRITE_REFUSED`), and leaves `verify_manifest` verifying each addendum only as its own standalone manifest without merging verdicts across manifests. `rdlab pilot-b compare-arms PARENT ADDENDUM --reader nemotron --out PATH` writes a markdown comparison report with per-pair verdicts, a cross-table, exact-agreement counts, per-set reader pass at each setting, optional completion tokens and wall time, and length stops reported separately from verdicts (their own count per setting and their own row and column in the cross-table, never counted as a verdict or folded into disagree, with agreement rates labelled over all pairs and over pairs with a verdict at both settings). `DEFAULT_PILOT_B_MODEL_PINS["reader_1"]` (Nemotron) now pins `think="medium"` (citing Ollama v0.40.9 `model/renderers/nemotron3nano.go:30, 86-87`: `"medium"` is an effort hint the template knows, not a cap), while `verify_manifest` continues to accept r3's `think: true` manifest unchanged.

- **reader_3 (Mistral Small 3.2) answers directly (`think: false`); the manifest records each reader's think setting.** Mistral Small 3.2 has no thinking mode in Ollama, so a `think: true` request would fail on every call; the reader_3 pin now says `think=False`, and the adapter sends exactly the pin's setting (reader_1 and reader_2 keep `think: true`). The value manifest's reader rows gain `think`, and `verify_manifest` checks each recorded reader's `think` against its pin (a manifest that records none, or one that records a deliberating Mistral, fails). Manifests made before this change record no `think` and so fail the verifier until re-made; none is committed. `run_ollama_calibration_calls` takes the model's pinned `think` (it was hard-coded `true`), so calibrating Mistral sends `think: false`; an explicit value that differs from the pin is refused (`PILOT_B_CALIBRATE_THINK_OFF_FORBIDDEN` for a thinking model, `PILOT_B_CALIBRATE_THINK_MISMATCH` otherwise). The prompt, `PROMPT_SHA256`, `parse_answer`, `CONTROL_PAIRS` and the other pins are untouched.

- **Pilot A: the 12 floor and ceiling cells are buildable, on role-set d29499f.** `role-set` is re-pinned to `d29499f77a81bda144ebd51885f3c167a04667aa` (role-set #122; `pyproject.toml`, `uv.lock`, the adapter's `ROLE_SET_COMMIT`, the registry citation in `map.py`). The adapter no longer hard-codes the claim question: it reads the shortcut's `from` operator in role-set's `REGISTRY` and builds an exact (`=`) item with the claim question and a floor (`>=`) or ceiling (`<=`) item with the set question ("Which of them could be ...?"), because a claim cannot separate "at least k" from "exactly k" while the set of possible groups can. It filters the t10_variety catalogue by question, `ifs` and operator, and passes `target_shortcut` to `build_t10` for the set cells only; the exact cells keep the untargeted build, so their items and proofs are byte-identical to before (a pinned hash in the tests). A role-set without `target_shortcut` is refused when the generator is built (`ROLE_SET_PIN_LACKS_TARGET_SHORTCUT`). `examples/t10-pilot-a.map.yaml` now has 24 buildable cells (capacity 5 each) and no `role-set PR pending` cells; to keep its splits (train 48, eval 12) and shares, the 12 exact cells' targets are 3 (were 5) and the 12 new cells' targets are 2, total 60. The receipts of the exact cells name the new generator commit, which is the only field of theirs that changes.
- **certify-values: the licence-registry gate, a registry sha in the manifest, and a role fallback that refuses.** `rdlab pilot-b certify-values` now requires `--licence-registry <absolute path>` and, before any Ollama call, validates exactly the reader pins that will run against it, so a real certify-values run refuses (`PILOT_B_LICENCE_NOT_IN_REGISTRY`) when rnd's registry lacks a reader's licence entry (until now only `rdlab models --verify-live` did). The manifest records `licence_registry_sha256`, and `verify_manifest` requires it; `rdlab pilot-b verify-values-manifest --licence-registry <path>` recomputes it from the file. `OllamaBlindReaderAdapter` built without a role now maps the model family to its role (nemotron to reader_1, granite to reader_2, mistral to reader_3) and refuses any other family with `PILOT_B_UNKNOWN_READER_FAMILY`, instead of defaulting to reader_1. Earlier manifests carry no registry sha and no longer verify.

- **reader_3: Mistral Small 3.2 24B (Apache-2.0) as the c2 second-reader family; no run yet.** `DEFAULT_PILOT_B_MODEL_PINS` gains `reader_3` (`mistral-small3.2:24b`, manifest digest `5a408ab5...`), `rdlab pilot-b certify-values --readers` accepts `mistral`, and the manifest verifier allows a reader_3 that ran. A manifest made by the other two readers still verifies. The licence registry needs the entry `mistral-small-3.2-24b-hf-card`, so a real (non-stub) run refuses until rnd's registry carries it.

- **control pairs v2: six arguable pairs replaced; earlier control results don't carry over.** A control pair must be compatible beyond argument, so the six whose key a careful reader could rightly dispute (chromoly then ferritic steel, a carbide coating then ceramic, docked then moored, sealed then waterproof, acoustic then sonic, a dry gas seal then mechanical seal) are replaced by chromoly then alloy steel, an alumina coating then ceramic, a steel hull then metal, IP67 then dust-tight, R-134a then HFC-134a, and a silicon-carbide seal face then ceramic. The other fourteen, the 0.80 threshold and the pass rule are unchanged. The manifest records `control_pairs_sha256`, and `verify_manifest` recomputes it, so a manifest made under the old table fails verification.

- **certification asks its own question.** The first full run showed the triad blind-read prompt (does the claim follow from the material?) makes a reader answer "unsupported" to a compatible but unstated claim (ester then synthetic), so it cannot tell a contradiction from a gap. `rdlab pilot-b certify-values` now asks: "Statement 1: The <attribute> of <entity> is <v1>. Statement 2: ... Could both statements be true of the same <entity> at the same time?", answered with exactly one word, `compatible` / `incompatible` / `unsure`, parsed strictly (case and surrounding whitespace ignored; anything else is a parse error and counts toward the error stop). A pair is a contradiction only on `incompatible`; a control pair passes on `compatible` or `unsure`. The 20 control pairs are unchanged. Same pins, `think` on, `num_predict` 3072, `num_ctx` 4096. The manifest records the sha256 of the prompt text, and `verify_manifest` rejects a manifest made with any other prompt. `verify_manifest` also recomputes each reader's control passes from the pairs and enforces the 0.80 threshold itself, instead of trusting the recorded `passed`. The real reader adapter gains `chat_raw` for callers that build their own messages. The CLI no longer writes the manifest a second time.

- **generator: every categorical value set sits on one axis and its values exclude each other.** An unsupported claim swaps the true value for another from the same set, so it is a contradiction only if both cannot be true. An audit of all 50 value sets (10 domains x 5) found most of them mixed an axis (a material beside a type, a mode beside a sub-process) or held a class beside its member (`synthetic` beside `ester`, `mineral` beside `paraffinic`, `air` beside `nitrogen`, `grounded` in two transformer sets), which makes an `unsupported` claim true and its key wrong (Pilot B stub 10d). All 50 sets were rewritten as 5 values on one declared axis (`VALUE_SET_AXES`); the 10 state sets hold top-level operating modes only, and no value sits in two sets of a domain. Some attribute names changed with their axis (for example `grid status` is now `turbine mode`, `valve state` is `pump state`), so facts and tables generated from a seed differ from earlier builds. `VALUE_SUBSUMPTIONS` records the known overlaps as a regression lint, `EXCLUSIVE_BY_MEANING` the close pairs reviewed with a reason, and `tests/test_pilot_b_value_sets.py` fails on a covering value beside its member, on one value spelled inside another, and on a value in two sets of a domain.
- **The exclusivity guard is not enforced yet.** The lint above only catches overlaps someone has written down; new or replacement values get no protection from it. The enforcing guard is the certification below, and it does not switch on until the certification PR (the manifest from a real run on the readers, `is_disjoint_value` reading it, and a CI gate that every set is certified with a matching hash). Until then, do not read this change as protection it does not give.
- **value certification tooling, with no certification claimed.** `pilot_b/value_certification.py`: `rdlab pilot-b certify-values` asks two local reader families whether every directed pair in a set (20 for 5 values) reads as a contradiction, one reader loaded at a time, with `--num-ctx`, `--num-predict`, `--readers`, a calibration of the first calls that stops the run when the projected time passes `--wall-cap-s`, and an error-rate stop (`--error-rate-stop`, 2% by default). It writes a content-hashed manifest of the admitted sets and a review queue of the others. `rdlab pilot-b verify-values-manifest` loads a manifest and fails when any set is uncertified or its hash no longer matches. Tested with fake readers; no manifest is committed.
- **certification: provenance, partial results, unload.** `verify_manifest` now requires a complete run, at least two readers from distinct families whose name, digest and family equal the pinned reader models in `DEFAULT_PILOT_B_MODEL_PINS` (a fake reader cannot match), and verdict keys equal to the manifest's reader names. A run that stops (error rate, wall cap) writes its manifest marked `"complete": false` with the stop code, the review queue, and `<queue>.failures.jsonl` (reader, pair, done_reason, completion_tokens, the tail of the raw text, or the error code); every exit path unloads the reader it loaded; the calibration projection also runs when one reader's jobs equal `--calibration-calls`. Defaults `--num-predict 3072`, `--num-ctx 4096` (thinking tokens count against `num_predict`; measured up to 2,019 in stub 10d); `--num-ctx` is a cap.
- **certification: length routing and a negative control.** A `length` or `max_num_ctx_exceeded` stop means not certified: the pair goes to the review queue with its reason and the failing call is logged, but it does not count toward `--error-rate-stop`, which counts only transport and parse errors. Before its certification calls each reader answers 20 compatible pairs (a member then its class, a synonym, an implication) through the same prompt and settings; it passes with at least 16 of 20 answers that are not "unsupported" (`--control-pass-threshold`, default 0.80; length stops and errors fail). If a reader fails, the run certifies nothing: the manifest says `"control_failed": true`, every set goes to the review queue, and the reader is unloaded. `manifest["control"]` records each reader's passes, threshold and every pair's verdict, done_reason and tokens, and `verify_manifest` rejects a manifest with no passing control.
- **reports record the render-failure gate.** `pilot_b_report.json` carries `render_failure_rate` and `max_render_failure_rate` (schema updated).
- Four more sets re-cut after the Argon review: reactors/cladding metal, compressors/rotor coating, boilers/tube alloy, chillers/condenser cooling (they need re-certifying).
- **render: the value-before-entity check is dropped, and intervals may be written two ways.** One entity per sentence already stops a value binding to the wrong entity. An interval may be "between <lo> and <hi>" or "from <lo> to <hi>". The writer prompts ask for "between" and for a direct verb (is, operates in, uses, has) on exact facts, never a hedged link such as "characterized by" or "associated with".
- **gate: render failures are their own rate.** They no longer count against every verdict class: class rates are over the triads that rendered, and `max_render_failure_rate` (default 0.50) halts the build with `PILOT_B_RENDER_FAILURE_RATE_ABOVE_CEILING`.
- **`mutate_fact_value` refuses instead of falling back.** A value outside its own attribute's set raises `PILOT_B_NO_EXCLUSIVE_VALUE`; the cross-attribute swap and the `non-<v>` / `uncoated-<v>` strings are gone.

- **Python: `requires-python` is `>=3.13` (was `>=3.12,<3.13`); Python 3.12 is dropped.** 3.15 is the main target (`.python-version`); CI runs the suite on 3.15 (`test`, with the Atlas check), 3.13 (`test-py313`) and 3.14 (`test-py314`), each from `uv sync --frozen`. Coverage (floor 90) and a Codecov upload run on the 3.15 leg only; the 3.13 and 3.14 legs run the tests without coverage. Every action is pinned by commit SHA with a version comment, at its current major (checkout v7.0.1, setup-uv v10.3.2, setup-node v7.1.0, codecov-action v7.1.1, read 2026-10-25). `role-set` is re-pinned to its commit that supports `>=3.13` (`d105f3d`, role-set PR #121; to be re-pointed to the merge commit). The Part F parity test now expects `>=3.13`. On 3.15, PyYAML 6.0.3 installs from its source distribution until it publishes cp315 wheels.

- sealing: the per-build seed is kept in the sealed store.
- sealing: the render inputs are validated at write and at verify, the report is cross-checked against them, and the schema maps are closed.
- sealing: more render-input rules (unique digests, constant call fields, catalogue domains, regular whitespace) and a coverage check on the report.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-10-25

### Added

- Pilot B builder (`reasoning_dataset_lab.pilot_b`):
  - **Part A (`fact_tables.py`):** Typed atomic fact tables (`number`, `date`, `interval`, `state`, `attribute`) with `closure` tags (`open` or `closed`), contrast triad construction (`supported`, `unsupported`, `cannot_tell`), and code-proved keys (`fact_restatement`, `disjoint_mutation`, `interval_interior_refinement`, `open_excerpt_unmentioned_attribute`).
  - **Part B (`rendering.py`):** Writer adapter protocol, deterministic writer and adversarial fakes (`FlippedBoundWriterFake`, `RelationalSwapWriterFake`, `UnlistedNumberWriterFake`, `UnlistedProperNounWriterFake`), sentence-level fact binding, polarity and quantifier direction checks (`at least` vs `at most`, negation, `only`, `between`), ungrounded number/proper-noun refusals, and structural token whitelisting (`DESIGN.md` ruling 8a, §11 step 3, §2.4).
  - **Part C (`unanimity.py`):** Blind-reader adapter protocol, key-free blind-read prompts, two-family unanimity gate, whole-triad pass/fail routing to `review_queue.jsonl`, hard refusal on same-family self-verification, reader family collision, Muse-Glimmer, and Argon/Gemini, and `min_pass_rate_per_class: 0.50` build halt (`DESIGN.md` ruling 2, ruling 8c, §6).
  - **Part D (`waves_and_sealing.py`):** Single-model wave runner (`render`, `word`, `reader_1`, `reader_2`) enforcing non-interleaved wave execution and per-call thinking token caps (`ruling 8b`), seeded item shuffling, aggregate-only judgment provenance (`§2.1`), external `keys.sealed.jsonl`, and content-based plus field-name sealing verification (`ruling 5a`).
  - **Part E (`models_and_build.py`, `ollama.py`, and CLI):** Pinned Ollama tags and manifest/layer digests for `qwen3.6:35b`, `nemotron-3.5-lightning:30b`, and `granite4.2:8b` with configurable `licence_id` checked against `licences/registry.json`, real loopback Ollama writer and blind-reader adapters (`OllamaWriterAdapter`, `OllamaBlindReaderAdapter`, interlock #140 loopback guard, pre-call `/api/tags` digest check with no auto-pull, per-wave `keep_alive: 0` model unload, and `is_fake` sealing guard), `rdlab models --verify-live`, `rdlab pilot-b --stub N`, and the Pilot B build report (`coverage`, `key_proof_rate: 1.0`, `gate_pass_rate_per_class`, per-wave timing/tokens, 50-triad full-build estimate, and 20-item human sample).

### Changed

- **Part F (`pyproject.toml`):** Narrow `requires-python` to `">=3.12,<3.13"` to match `role-set` at its pinned commit `2b26840`, with a regression test verifying that the lab never allows a Python version that `role-set` refuses.
